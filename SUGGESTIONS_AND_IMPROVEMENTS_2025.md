## ⚡ 2. Strategy to Avoid "Tutorial Hell" & Optimize Time

You have a 3-4 hour daily limit. The biggest risk you face is getting stuck endlessly consuming content without building capabilities.

### A. Project-Driven Learning (The "Apply-First" Method)

- **Problem:** Watching Khan Academy and MIT OCW videos is passive. It feels like learning, but it doesn't stick unless used.
- **Solution:** For every 1 hour of video/reading, you must spend 2 hours coding or simulating.
  - *Example (Bio):* Don't just watch videos on the Central Dogma. Write a Python script that takes a DNA string, transcribes it to RNA, and translates it to an Amino Acid sequence.
  - *Example (Quantum):* Don't just memorize unitary matrices. Immediately code them in Qiskit and visualize the Bloch sphere.

### B. Synergy Between Tracks (Killing Two Birds with One Stone)

You want to do ML, Quantum, Bio, and Algo Trading. You **cannot** do these completely separately.

- **Merge ML + Algo Trading:** Use your Algorithmic Trading track as your playground for Machine Learning. Time-series forecasting for stocks is excellent practice for sequential ML models (RNNs/LSTMs/Transformers).

- **Merge ML + Bio:** Apply PyTorch/JAX to biological sequence data (like RNA-seq).

## 🛠️ 3. Concrete Daily Workflow Improvements (For the 3-4 Hour Limit)

1. **The 90/90 Rule:** Pick exactly **one** deep-focus task for the first 90 minutes of your block. If you are learning Linear Algebra today, do not look at Biology during that 90 minutes.
2. **Spaced Repetition (Crucial):** With so many fields, you *will* forget things. Use **Anki** daily for 15-20 minutes. Put math formulas, biological pathways (like mTOR/AMPK), and Python syntax into flashcards.
3. **Weekly "Synthesis" Day:** Spend your Sunday 3-hour block not learning anything new, but combining what you learned. Write a blog post, a GitHub README, or a short markdown summary of how your ML learning this week applies to your Bio learning.
